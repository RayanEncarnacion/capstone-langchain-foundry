# Northstar Learning Program Overview

Northstar is a fictional self-directed learning program used for practice and demonstration. It helps learners turn a broad subject into a small weekly plan, study in focused sessions, check their understanding, and record concrete next steps.

## Program structure

The standard Northstar cycle lasts **six weeks**. New cycles begin on the first Monday of each month, but a learner may start independently at any time. Starting outside the monthly cycle means that live group activities might not align with the learner's current module.

Each cycle contains three stages:

1. **Foundation** — identify essential vocabulary and build a basic mental model.
2. **Applied practice** — solve small problems and explain decisions.
3. **Review and transfer** — revisit weak areas and apply the ideas in a new scenario.

The stages are not separate tracks. They are the recommended sequence inside every learning cycle. Learners who already understand the foundations may spend less time in the first stage, but they should still complete the initial diagnostic.

## Weekly expectations

The recommended weekly commitment is **three to five hours**. This usually means four to eight focused study sessions plus one weekly review. Northstar does not require daily attendance, and missing one day does not reset progress.

A learner is considered active when they have completed at least one study session, assessment attempt, or task update during the previous seven days.

## Core records

Northstar distinguishes between three types of information:

- **Learning notes** explain program concepts and policies.
- **Preferences** describe how a learner likes to study, such as a preferred session duration.
- **Tasks** are concrete actions with a title, status, and optional due date.

Preferences should be remembered only when the learner states them explicitly. The system should not infer sensitive traits from conversation history.

## Official-source rule

Documents marked `status: current` and `authority: official` are the authoritative source for present-day program rules. Archived documents are useful for historical questions but must not override current policies. Community notes can provide tips, but they are not policy.

## Getting started

To begin a cycle, a learner should:

1. Choose a subject and a measurable six-week outcome.
2. Complete the short diagnostic.
3. Select the first week's objectives.
4. Create no more than five active tasks.
5. Schedule the first focused study session.

The program favors small, observable progress over elaborate planning.
