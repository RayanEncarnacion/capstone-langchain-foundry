# Current Frequently Asked Questions

## Do I have to study every day?

No. Northstar recommends three to five hours per week, but it does not require daily attendance. Consistent weekly progress matters more than maintaining a daily streak.

## How long is a normal session?

The standard session is 25 focused minutes followed by a 5-minute break. A 50-minute extended block with a 10-minute break is also supported.

## How many active tasks can I have?

Five. Tasks in `planned` or `in_progress` status are active. Complete an existing task before creating a sixth active one.

## What score passes a module checkpoint?

The current threshold is 80 percent. Older handbooks used a different value, but those rules no longer apply.

## Can I retry a checkpoint?

Yes. There are three attempts. After the second unsuccessful attempt, wait 24 hours before the third attempt and complete targeted review.

## Are live sessions required?

No. The Wednesday question session and Friday review room are optional. Recordings or written summaries are normally available within 24 hours.

## Can the assistant remember my preferred study duration?

Yes, if you state the preference explicitly. You should also be able to view, change, or delete that remembered preference. A one-time session length should not automatically become a permanent preference.

## Can the assistant create tasks automatically?

It may propose a task, but creating or modifying the stored task requires approval. The assistant should display the proposed title, due date and source before execution.

## What happens when current sources disagree?

Prefer the most recently updated official document that directly governs the topic. If the conflict cannot be resolved from metadata and content, explain the disagreement instead of choosing silently.

## What happens when an archived document disagrees with a current one?

Use the current official document for present-day questions. Use the archived document only when the user explicitly asks about the former policy or historical practice.
