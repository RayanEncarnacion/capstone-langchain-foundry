# Learning Tracks and Module Selection

Northstar offers three learning tracks. A track describes the kind of practice a learner needs most; it does not indicate rank or status.

## Explorer track

The **Explorer** track is for learners who are new to a subject. It emphasizes terminology, examples, concept maps, and short recall exercises. A typical week contains two reading sessions, two recall sessions, and one guided practice session.

Choose Explorer when the initial diagnostic score is below 50 percent or when the learner cannot yet explain the subject's core vocabulary.

## Builder track

The **Builder** track is for learners who know the fundamentals but need practical experience. It emphasizes small projects, comparison of alternatives, debugging, and explanation of design choices.

Choose Builder when the diagnostic score is between 50 and 79 percent or when the learner understands definitions but struggles to apply them.

## Navigator track

The **Navigator** track is for review, transfer, and exam-style preparation. It emphasizes mixed scenarios, time-limited practice, error analysis, and connecting ideas across modules.

Choose Navigator when the diagnostic score is 80 percent or higher and the learner can already solve ordinary practice problems.

## Switching tracks

A learner may switch tracks at the start of any week. Switching does not erase completed tasks, assessments, or notes. The learner should record a brief reason for the change so later progress can be interpreted correctly.

Do not automatically move a learner to another track after one score. Review at least two signals, such as assessment performance and the learner's explanation of where they are struggling.

## Relationship to program stages

Tracks and stages are different:

- A **track** changes the style and difficulty of practice.
- A **stage** describes progression from foundation to application to review.

For example, a learner can complete the Foundation stage while using the Builder track if they already know much of the terminology but need structured implementation practice.
